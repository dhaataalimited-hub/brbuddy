How it works

Walk away. Your agent keeps going.

Long agent runs and a Mac that dozes off don't mix. Neither does leaving your screen open for anyone walking past.

Set it and walk away

Connect your agents once in Settings. While one is working, BRBuddy keeps the Mac awake by itself and lets it rest again when the agent's done.

BRB covers the screen

Three minutes after you stop typing, or straight away with ⌃⌥⌘B, a companion covers every display and shows what your agent is doing.

Come back and unlock

Press any key and use Touch ID or your password. Too many wrong tries, or an hour away, and it hands over to the real macOS lock screen.

Features

Small app. Thinks of the details.

Keeps your Mac awake

Only while an agent works or needs you, then lets the Mac sleep. Or flip it on yourself from the paw in the menu bar.

Covers every display

App switching, Force Quit and quit shortcuts are blocked while BRB is up, so the keyboard does nothing until you unlock.

Touch ID or password

Checked by macOS itself. BRBuddy never sees, stores or logs your password.

Knows your agent

Working, needs you, or finished: the menu and the away screen keep you posted. Click an agent in the menu to jump to its terminal tab.

Walk-away lock

Pick your iPhone or Apple Watch. When it leaves the desk, the room or the building, BRBuddy starts BRB or locks the Mac.

Terminal on the away screen

Optional and view-only: watch a tmux pane, a Terminal, iTerm2 or Ghostty window, or a feed of your agent's steps from across the room.

Battery aware

On a MacBook the away screen shows charge and time left. Your companion cheers when you plug in, sweats when the battery's low, moves in slow motion in Low Power Mode, and holds still on battery to save power.

Updates itself

Signed and notarized releases, checked once a day. New versions are verified before they install.

Works with

One click to connect your command-line agents

Claude Code Codex Cursor Gemini CLI Antigravity Grok

Setup adds one small entry to the agent's own settings file and keeps a backup next to it. It only reports what the agent is doing, can't change anything it does, and never sends your prompts.

Privacy

Nothing leaves your Mac.

What BRBuddy does with your data

  • Collects nothing. No account, no analytics, no server. The only thing it fetches is the update feed.
  • Your password is checked by macOS. BRBuddy never stores or logs it.
  • Permissions are asked for only when you turn on the feature that needs them: Accessibility for the terminal view, Bluetooth for the walk-away lock.
  • The terminal view is off by default, because anyone at your Mac can read it.
  • Open source under the MIT licence. Read every line.

What it isn't

  • An away screen, not a lock. It keeps passers-by off your keyboard and screen.
  • Anything running as you, including your agent, can close it, and a restart ends it. Holding the power button still restarts the Mac.
  • When you need real security, lock the Mac, or leave “Locking for real” on so BRBuddy hands over to the macOS lock screen.
  • Closing the lid on battery still sleeps the Mac. That's macOS.

FAQ

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. No subscription, no in-app purchase, no account. It's open source under the MIT licence.

Can my company use it?

Yes, free. The MIT licence covers companies as well as individuals, no licence fee or sign-up. If your team relies on BRBuddy, we'd love to hear from you at hello@dhaataa.com: feedback, a feature your team needs, or support for keeping it going.

Do I need an AI agent to use it?

No. Turn on Keep Mac awake from the paw in the menu bar and use BRB whenever you step away. Agents just make it smarter about when to stay awake.

Which agents does it work with?

Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini CLI, Antigravity and Grok, each set up with one button in Settings. Desktop chat apps can't report their status; their command-line agents can.

Is it secure?

It's an away screen, not a lock: it stops someone at your desk from using your keyboard or reading your screen, and unlocking needs Touch ID or your password. It isn't a replacement for the macOS lock screen, so it hands over to that after too many wrong tries or an hour away (you can change both).

My Mac still went to sleep. Why?

Check the paw in the menu bar is filled in, and that your agent is set up in Settings → Agents. Closing the lid on battery always sleeps the Mac; that's macOS.

Will it slow my Mac down or drain the battery?

It's a small native app. On battery the companion's body holds still and only its face moves, and it slows right down in Low Power Mode.

How do I uninstall it?

First press Remove next to each agent in Settings → Agents (a backup of each agent's original settings stays next to it). Then quit BRBuddy and drag it to the Bin. With Homebrew: brew uninstall --zap brbuddy.

Where do I report a bug or ask for a feature?

GitHub Issues. Anything else: hello@dhaataa.com. Security problems: please report them privately (see SECURITY.md).
